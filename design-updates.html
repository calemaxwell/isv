Before the options

The current module is a table wearing editorial clothes. Six things are wrong with it, and two of them are data problems rather than design problems.

Everything weighs the same

Four rows at identical size. Nothing tells you which one matters, so you read all of it or none of it. Most people choose none.

The chip is noise

"Governance, compliance and risk" four times. A label that never varies carries no information and costs a line of vertical space each time.

Forty percent of the canvas is empty

Everything is jammed into the left two thirds. The width is doing nothing.

Summaries flatten the rhythm

Every row is title plus two lines of body. Uniform blocks read as a wall, not a list.

Nothing to land on

No numeral, no colour, no scale change, no imagery. The eye has no entry point.

It tells and never asks

Four facts and not one action. A member reads it and moves on, which is the opposite of what the module is for.

Two of these are data fixes, and they matter more than the visual treatment. First, the Principal's four updates are all one category. Give each persona a genuine spread across compliance, people, finance and strategy and the colour system below starts doing real work. Second, an update with nowhere to go is a dead end. Every item needs a destination, even if it is only the resource it changed.

Recommendation

A over the others, with two things borrowed.

Build A, take the "you last visited here" marker from B, and take the category colour system across all of them. A gives you hierarchy, an action and a use for the empty width. B's marker is what turns a list into an answer. The colour system only pays off once each persona has a genuine spread of categories, which is a data change worth making regardless: right now the Principal's four updates are all governance, and no amount of design fixes that.
